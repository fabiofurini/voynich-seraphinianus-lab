How to read it EVERYONE

European alphabets usually have 20–30 letters with a few very common vowels. The Voynich working set is similar in size, so frequencies alone cannot tell us whether a glyph is a letter, a syllable or part of a larger sign. The rare glyphs may be variants, errors, or symbols with a special use.

Experiment 3 · linguistics

The word machine.

Six common word starts, six common endings. Every one of the 36 combinations exists in the text. Darker cells are more frequent. Click a cell.

How often each start + ending occurs

25,373 words from 2,952 legible paragraph lines.

Click a cell to read the word.

A slot machine?

The qok row is spread almost evenly over all six endings. Any start seems to fit any ending, as if words were assembled from independent parts.

Or a grammar?

The ch and sh rows strongly prefer edy and ol: 383 chedy against 14 chain. Parts are not independent, which is what agreement or morphology would do.

Why this matters LINGUISTICS

Gordon Rugg (2004) and Timm & Schinner (2020) showed that simple mechanical procedures can produce text with many Voynich-like statistics. A generator that looks right is not proof that the manuscript was made that way, but it raises the bar: any claim of meaning has to explain something a generator cannot. The “depend on the previous word” option mimics the strong boundary dependency we measured between neighbouring words.

Experiment 5 · the decipherment game

Be the decipherer.

Give each Voynich glyph a letter and watch 60 frequent Voynich words turn into “Latin” or “Italian”. Then let the computer cheat.

0 of 60 words found in the dictionary

The trap every decipherer falls into

With 17 symbols to assign and a free choice of key, a search always finds some “words”. Press the search button and count them. That is how many published decipherments work: they fit the key to the text and then report the matches.

Our real test avoided the trap by fixing the key on some pages and checking it on others it had never seen. The best keys found only 1.1% Latin words and 7.0% Italian words there, while the same method recovered 100% of a genuine enciphered text.

StatisticYour textVoynich (ZL3b)Changed by a simple cipher?

The sample is a short Latin herbal passage written for this demo. Statistics on a few hundred letters are noisy; paste a longer text for stabler numbers.

What the comparison shows STATISTICS

A one-to-one substitution renames letters but keeps every frequency, every repeated letter and every word length. So if your language's numbers are far from the Voynich's, no simple substitution of your language can produce the Voynich. This is why researchers turned to verbose ciphers, syllabaries and abbreviation systems, which do change these numbers.

Single-glyph entropy is computed from frequencies only. Conditional entropy on short texts is strongly biased downward, so we do not compare it with the Voynich here; see the matched, held-out estimates on the results page.
